The Gap Nobody Talks About

Most families face this alone. They don't have to.

The facilities are often full. The ones with availability have time limits, and the ones without limits are simply out of reach financially. Even the resources that do exist tend to feel impersonal — built for a general case, not for your family, your home, your reality.

So families bring their loved ones home and figure it out. They rent equipment they don't know how to set up. They rearrange furniture at midnight. They absorb the coordination weight that nobody prepared them for, on top of everything else they're already carrying.

There is a better way. Families are not helpless in these situations. There are real options, practical solutions, and a clear path forward. They just need someone who knows how to find it.

Our Methodology

The OASIS Method

A structured approach to stabilizing and optimizing home life during medical transitions. Not a checklist. A system built around your family's actual situation.

O
Operational Assessment
We assess the full picture — the home, the care needs, the family dynamic, and the gaps between where you are and where you need to be.
A
Actionable Stabilization
Immediate, practical fixes. Safety, workflow, equipment, and schedule — addressed in the right order so the household finds its footing fast.
S
Seamless Integration
Medical instructions translated into real daily routines. The home oriented for both comfort and care. Equipment sourced, set up, and explained.
I
Informed Support
The family aligned on a shared plan. Vendors coordinated. Questions answered. No one left holding information nobody else has.
S
Sustained Navigation
Ongoing support as the situation evolves. Because a medical transition rarely unfolds on a fixed timeline — and neither does our presence.

Why This Work Exists

"What we needed was someone to step in and handle the logistics. That person didn't exist for us."

Daily, Founder · Gilt Consulting Partners

When my grandmother's health began to decline in my mid-twenties, our family did what most families do. We figured it out as we went. She was the matriarch — strong, independent, the center of everything. Watching her lose that independence was one of the hardest things we've been through as a family.

We looked into facilities. Some were full. Others had time limits. The ones that could actually accommodate her needs were simply out of reach financially. Even the resources that seemed promising felt impersonal — built for a general case, not for her, not for our family.

So we brought her home. We hired a nurse for a few hours a day. We rearranged furniture. We ordered equipment we didn't know how to set up. I was 24 years old, absorbing a coordination weight nobody had prepared me for — on top of everything else our family was already carrying.

What we needed was someone to step in and handle the logistics. To translate the medical instructions into a real daily routine. To get the family aligned on a plan so the weight wasn't distributed unevenly across everyone who loved her. That person didn't exist for us. Gilt Consulting Partners exists so they exist for you.
